An internal inspection often begins with a simple question: can the team see enough of an asset to decide what needs closer attention? Confined-space drone inspection can collect visual evidence from suitable enclosed areas while reducing some requirements for people to enter or work at height.

Orbit operates Flyability Elios 3 for appropriate internal inspection assignments in Kuwait. A useful scope starts with the asset and inspection objective, then establishes access, operating conditions and the evidence required. Explore our [confined-space inspection service](/services/confined-space/) for that discussion.

## Where an internal drone can help

Candidate assets may include emptied tanks, silos, enclosed structures and other industrial spaces where the inspection team needs views of accessible internal surfaces. Suitability depends on the actual geometry, condition and operating environment; the asset label alone cannot establish feasibility.

For a Kuwait facility, explain whether the question concerns a visible lining condition, accumulated material, an obstruction or a particular feature requiring review. Provide earlier inspection references where available. This helps the team focus capture on useful observations instead of returning a long video with no clear inspection purpose.

## What Elios 3 brings to the task

Flyability describes Elios 3 as a caged, collision-tolerant drone designed for indoor work. Its LiDAR, cameras and simultaneous localisation and mapping, or SLAM, support navigation where satellite positioning is unavailable. A live three-dimensional view helps the pilot relate the aircraft to its surroundings. These capabilities are described on the [manufacturer’s Elios 3 page](https://www.flyability.com/elios-3).

The cage does not make every opening passable or every contact harmless. Review access dimensions, internal obstructions and the route to the target. Discuss how findings will be located in the asset so that an engineer can return to the relevant area later.

## Agree the evidence before the visit

A useful inspection delivery might combine selected images, video references and a register of observations. Specify what the receiving team needs to review and the naming convention it already uses.

| Report item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Asset and inspection date | Identifies which condition record is being reviewed |
| Feature or location reference | Connects an observation to the appropriate asset area |
| Image or video reference | Lets the reviewer inspect the source evidence |
| Coverage and visibility notes | Distinguishes observed areas from gaps or obscured surfaces |
| Follow-up questions | Separates captured evidence from decisions needing specialist review |

Agree any spatial export separately. A navigation map or an image location is not automatically an engineering survey, dimensioned model or certified condition assessment.

## Understand what visual evidence cannot establish

Images can show visible surface conditions. They cannot, on their own, establish remaining wall thickness, hidden defects, material strength or fitness for continued service. The responsible inspection team determines whether further testing or physical access is necessary.

Flyability offers additional payloads for tasks such as ultrasonic thickness measurement and specialised surveying. Those options are [separate parts of its payload range](https://knowledge.flyability.com/getting-started/start-with-payloads-and-accessories). Any thermal, thickness or other specialist measurement requirement must be confirmed with Orbit for the specific assignment. Owning Elios 3 does not establish availability of every payload or method.

## Plan the atmosphere, heat and visibility

Share the asset’s recent operating state, contents, residue and known hazards with the site’s responsible personnel. They must establish the required isolation, atmospheric assessment, access controls and authorisation. A drone visit is part of that process, not a substitute for it.

Elios 3 is not ATEX certified. Flyability explicitly notes that adding its flammable-gas sensor does not confer ATEX certification. The [manufacturer’s gas-sensor announcement](https://www.flyability.com/news/flammable-gas-sensor-press-release) explains that distinction. Do not treat an internal inspection drone as approved for an explosive atmosphere.

For work in Kuwait, assess the actual temperature inside the asset and at the staging area. Heat retained after operation may matter as much as outdoor weather. Dust, residues and poor visibility also need review. Flyability’s [Elios 3 warning guidance](https://knowledge.flyability.com/aircraft/elios-3/elios-3-troubleshooting/elios-3-warning-messages) includes temperature and sensor warnings; the operational plan must follow the current instructions for the equipment used.

## Send a brief that supports a feasibility review

Include the asset type and location, drawings or access photographs, opening dimensions, target areas, inspection question, known hazards and proposed availability window. Identify the site contact, who will interpret the evidence, and any required reporting format. Agree how incomplete coverage and follow-up work will be recorded.

External surfaces may instead call for [visual inspection](/services/visual-inspection/). If the main requirement is a measurable spatial dataset, our [survey deliverables guide](/journal/drone-surveying-kuwait-deliverables/) explains the additional scoping questions. Choosing the right evidence at the start makes the resulting inspection easier to act on.
