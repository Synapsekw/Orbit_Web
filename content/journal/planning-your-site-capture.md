## Start with the decision

Before deciding how often to fly, decide who needs to see the results and what they need to understand. A client update, an engineering review, and a project film may use the same site, but they need different kinds of coverage.

## Agree the views that matter

Identify key work areas, milestones, and useful repeat viewpoints with the project team. A consistent view helps people compare change without having to reorient themselves every time. Leave room for additional views as the site develops.

## Connect the schedule to the build

Use the construction programme and reporting calendar as a starting point. Agree how the team will communicate milestone changes, site access requirements, and interruptions before they affect a capture visit.

## Plan the handover of information

Discuss how imagery will be named, organized, and shared. Clear dates, site areas, and capture references make a growing visual archive easier to navigate. Confirm the delivery formats and access arrangements your stakeholders need.

## Keep the final story in view

Progress documentation can also become a record of the entire project. Thinking about a future time-lapse or handover film from the beginning helps guide capture choices along the way.
