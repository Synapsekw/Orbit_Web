## Capture the beginning

The early stages of a site can feel unremarkable at the time. By completion, those same views provide a valuable contrast. Groundworks, structure, and finishing stages each contribute a different chapter.

## Keep returning to familiar views

Repeated viewpoints give the audience a clear visual reference as the build changes. Alongside those reference views, capture the distinctive moments and details that make the project its own.

## Think about the audience

An internal progress update needs a different rhythm from a public project film. Knowing the intended channels early helps the team capture the framing and variety needed for both.

## Make room for the finished project

A final visit can show the completed architecture in its surroundings. Pairing that footage with the construction archive turns a collection of individual updates into a connected story.

## Keep the archive useful

The film is one output. Organized photographs and footage also remain useful for future presentations, company portfolios, and the project team’s own record.
