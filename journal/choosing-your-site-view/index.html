SPATIAL PERSPECTIVE

One site. More than one way to understand it.

How photographs, spatial models, and immersive views support different conversations.

3 min readOrbit field notes · editorial draft
IN THIS NOTEBegin with contextAdd another angleChoose the right layerBring people into the spaceDefine the intended use

Begin with context

Aerial photographs are an accessible starting point for a site discussion. They show visible activity and the relationship between areas in a format that most stakeholders can read immediately.

Add another angle

A textured 3D model lets a viewer move around the captured scene. This can help when a single photograph obscures a relationship between structures or when the team needs to consider the site from a different perspective.

Choose the right layer

A point cloud presents sampled surface points. A digital surface model represents surface heights, including visible structures. These are different ways of examining a captured site; the most useful output depends on the question being asked.

Bring people into the space

A 360° walkthrough gives a remote viewer a way to look around from captured locations. It is useful when the surroundings of a viewpoint matter as much as the object in front of the camera.

Define the intended use

Visual communication and engineering measurement have different requirements. Discuss purpose, accuracy, coverage, and validation with the delivery team before capture. The interactive model in our spatial explorer illustrates the viewing experience; it is not a survey deliverable.
